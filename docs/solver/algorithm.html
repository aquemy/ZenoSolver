Algorithm

Bracket, link-forest DP, breakpoint search, and the non-symmetric pipeline

This page summarises how zeno computes a front. The proofs are in the theory note (§7 for the symmetric algorithm, §6.5 for the non-symmetric one), and each step links to its node of the blueprint.

The reformulation

A plan lands at least \(s = 2t - p\) times in central cities (Lemma 3.2). Under (S) and (A1), the front is reached by plans with exactly \(s\) landings, split among the planes as an admissible partition (Corollary 4.8): a partition \(X_1, \dots, X_p\) of a multiset \(X\) of cities with a parity matrix, that is, in every connected component of the plane–city graph, an even number of planes land an even number of times (Definition 2.2, Lemma 2.4). An admissible partition is flown with no waiting, in makespan \(2\max_k \delta(X_k)\) where \(\delta\) sums the durations (Theorem 4.1), and no plan does better (Theorem 4.7).

With integer durations divided by their gcd \(g\), let \[E(T) = \min\{c(X) : |X| = s,\ X_1,\dots,X_p \text{ admissible},\ \delta(X_k) \le T\ \forall k\}.\] \(E\) is non-increasing, and the front is the set of its breakpoints, \((E(T), 2gT)\) wherever \(E(T) < E(T-1)\) (Proposition 7.1). Only the levels \(T_{\rm lo} = d_{\min}\kappa \le T \le T_{\rm hi} = d_{\rm cheap}\kappa\) matter, with \(\kappa = \lceil s/p \rceil\) (Proposition 7.10): below, there is no plan; above, \(E\) is the cheapest cost \(s\,c_{\min}\).

Symmetric pipeline

  1. Reduce. Divide the durations by their gcd, merge equal cities and drop dominated ones (\(d_{i'} \le d_i\) and \(c_{i'} \le c_i\)), which changes neither \(E\) nor the front (Proposition 7.9). The kept cities form a staircase: durations strictly increasing, costs strictly decreasing (reduce, zeno/reduce.hpp).
  2. One-plane table. \(G[m][T]\), the cheapest \(m\) landings whose durations sum to at most \(T\) (Lemma 7.2). It is stored densely when small, otherwise as one Pareto staircase of (load, cost) per \(m\), so that memory does not depend on the magnitude of the durations (OnePlaneTable).
  3. Bracket each level. Three knapsacks over planes and landings combine gadgets:
    • \(L(T)\): single planes of any size, no admissibility. It is a lower bound on the cost of every plan of makespan at most \(2T\), whatever the central flights (lemma R, Lemma 3.4);
    • \(U(T)\): single planes of odd size, and pairs, two planes of even size linked at a city;
    • \(U_3(T)\): in addition, chains of three planes.
    \(L \le E \le U_3 \le U\) (Proposition 7.3). \(E = U\) when useful link trees have at most two planes, \(q_{\max} = \min(p, t-p+1) \le 2\), and \(E = U_3\) when \(q_{\max} \le 3\) (Proposition 7.4). A level is settled when the bracket closes.
  4. Exact DP on the open levels. On the levels where \(L < U_3\), the link-forest DP computes \(E(T)\) exactly (Theorem 7.5). A partition is admissible iff it has a link forest (Lemma 2.6): a forest on the planes whose edges carry city labels, each edge \(\{u, v\}\) labelled \(i\) using one landing in \(i\) of both \(X_u\) and \(X_v\), such that every plane keeps an odd number of free landings (Definition (link forest)). In the plan, a link at \(i\) is a passenger dropped off in \(i\) by one plane and picked up by the other. Trees have at most \(q_{\max}\) planes (Corollary 2.7). The DP roots every tree and combines child subtrees as Pareto staircases over \((\tau, \text{cost})\), \(\tau\) being the part of a plane’s load taken by its child edges, so its work depends on the staircase sizes and not on \(T\) (ForestProgram, zeno/forest.hpp). Because \(E\) is non-increasing, equal values at both ends of an interval fix it on the whole interval (monotone in --stats).
  5. Find the breakpoints. --levels sweep evaluates every level; --levels search splits every interval \((a, b)\) with \(E(a) > E(b)\) at up to threads evenly spaced levels evaluated in parallel. Pieces with equal ends are constant, so with \(r\) breakpoints it evaluates \(O(r \log(T_{\rm hi} - T_{\rm lo}))\) levels (breakpoints, zeno/breakpoints.hpp). The default, auto, sweeps up to \(2^{12}\) levels and searches beyond, which is why the time grows with the logarithm of the level range.
  6. Plans. For each point, the partition is rebuilt from the exact method’s choices, a link forest computed from it, and the plan of Theorem 4.1 built. Every plane first drops off one passenger per child edge (\(C_I \to b \to C_I\)), then flies its free landings as alternating crossings, then picks up the passenger its parent dropped (\(C_G \to a \to C_G\)). Every plan is re-simulated by checkPlan, which shares no code with the builder.
  7. Certificate. Each gap gets relaxed when \(L\) already reaches the cost at the level below the next point, forest otherwise (certificate format).

Why the 2016 solvers were wrong: they fixed the multiset of landings (a PPP) and scheduled it greedily, longest first onto the least-loaded plane. That is a heuristic, and deciding whether a fixed multiset can be flown within a makespan is strongly NP-complete (§5, Theorem 8.1). The link-forest DP does not fix the multiset. Its cost is pseudo-polynomial (Corollary 8.3).

Non-symmetric pipeline

When the durations to \(C_I\) (\(d\)) and to \(C_G\) (\(\bar d\)) differ, a landing’s weight depends on its role: a crossing \(C_I \to i \to C_G\) or back weighs \(d_i + \bar d_i\), a drop-off \(C_I \to i \to C_I\) weighs \(2d_i\), a pick-up \(C_G \to i \to C_G\) weighs \(2\bar d_i\). A role configuration assigns roles per plane, each plane flying an odd number of crossings, with drop-offs and pick-ups balanced city by city (Definition 4.2). A pick-up may have to wait for its drop-off, and that waiting can be harmful: the role loads alone do not give the front (Proposition 6.5). So zeno computes a certified bracket (solveBracket, zeno/ns.hpp):

  1. Upper points. Plans built from link forests in which every plane picks up at most once (or, by time reversal, drops off at most once), and from the cheapest balanced role configurations found in the gaps, all scheduled and simulated with their waiting. Every printed point is a real plan.
  2. Lower bounds per gap, for every plan of makespan below the next point:
    • relaxed: lemma R with weight \(\min(2d_i, 2\bar d_i)\) per landing. No assumption on central flights;
    • roles: the role relaxation (each plane an odd number \(\ge 1\) of crossings, all loads adding up to the crossing weight of the landings);
    • lagrangian: a Lagrangian relaxation of the per-city balance, with one multiplier per city;
    • balanced: the exact program over balanced role configurations, a DP over planes on the imbalance vector (Proposition 6.6), exponential in \(t - p\) and stopped at the roleStates and roleWork limits;
    • search: the exhaustive search over role configurations of cost below the gap’s cost, each with every drop-off order and drop-off-to-pick-up matching, the closed form of the template schedule (Lemma 6.3) giving its makespan. It stops at the searchWork limit. It is the only kind that closes gaps opened by harmful waiting.
    roles, lagrangian and balanced need (A1-NS) through Theorem 6.1: every plan is weakly dominated by a role configuration with the same cost and loads at most its makespan. search needs (A1-NS) through Theorem 6.9, schedulable domination: every plan is dominated by a role configuration whose template schedule is no later. Theorem 6.9 holds for every plan, also on instances with a city of \(d = \bar d = 0\), so no event-order assumption is needed.
  3. Report. The front is exact when the fastest makespan is certified and every gap is closed. Otherwise zeno lists each open gap with its proved lower bound and the reason it stays open, rather than print a front it cannot prove (command line). The refinement of open gaps stops after refineSeconds.

On 1,800 random tiny non-symmetric instances checked against the brute-force oracle, every bracket closes and equals the oracle’s front (README.md).

Complexity

From §8 of the note, with \(\hat m = s - p + 1\) (the largest group) and \(q_{\max} = \min(p, t-p+1)\):

Step Time
One-plane table \(G\), all levels \(O(n\,\hat m\,T_{\rm hi})\)
\(L\) and \(U\), per level \(O(p\,s^2 + n\,\hat m^2)\)
\(U_3\) chains, per level \(O(n^2\,\hat m\,s + p\,s^2)\)
Link-forest DP, per level \(O(n\,q_{\max}^2 s^2 T + p\,q_{\max} s^2)\)
Plan for one point \(O(s + n + p)\), up to union–find

These are the bounds of the dense algorithm. The staircase representations make the table and the forest DP independent of the magnitude of the durations in practice, and the breakpoint search evaluates \(O(r \log(T_{\rm hi} - T_{\rm lo}))\) levels instead of all of them. Measured scaling is on the results and size guidance pages.
