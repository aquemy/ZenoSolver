Results

Correctness checks and timings, computed from the committed baselines

Every number on this page is computed from the JSON baselines committed under bench/baselines/ when the site is built (site/gen_pages.py). None is typed by hand.

Apple M4 Max, 16 cores, macOS, Release builds; recorded 2026-11-23, 2026-11-24. Source: bench/baselines/*/apple-m4-max/.

Correctness

  • Golden fronts. zeno reproduces all 27 fronts of the brute-force oracle (golden suite). Five of them are instances on which the 2016 solvers were still wrong after their bookkeeping fixes, because their greedy schedule is not optimal (§5).
  • Every level. ut-zeno-core compares the value of every level, the bracket order \(L \le E \le U_3 \le U\) and the front with an independent enumeration oracle on random instances.
  • Non-symmetric. On 1,800 tiny random non-symmetric instances, every bracket closes and equals the oracle’s front.
  • Two solvers, one front. The Lean solver zeno-front, whose output is proved to be the front, gives the same front as zeno front on every suite instance (table below). CI compares the two on the golden, quick and three regime instances.
  • Certificates. CI writes certificates for the golden and random instances with zeno and has both checkers verify them. The Lean checker accepts the certificates of all 66 suite instances.

The rejected/ certificates of the test set are corrupted on purpose, so their rejection is the expected verdict. The one rejected random version 2 certificate is the Proposition 6.5 instance (v2-random/prop65); bench/README.md notes that it needs the search kind. The certificate zeno writes today for that instance closes two gaps with search, and zeno-check accepts it (Lean tools).

Component benchmarks and CI

Each algorithm component has a Google Benchmark executable (bm-core, bm-plan, bm-ns), with counters that explain its time (peak RSS, states, gap levels, certificate bytes) and complexity fits to compare with the bounds of the note. Their baselines are in bench/baselines/zeno/<machine>/, recorded while other builds ran, so differences below 20% are noise.

.github/workflows/bench.yml runs every component benchmark and the quick suite on pull requests and writes the comparison with bench/baselines/*/ci-ubuntu-24.04/ to the job summary, without blocking. A nightly run on the runner named by ZS_BENCH_RUNNER fails on a regression above ZS_BENCH_THRESHOLD (default 25%, with a 5 ms noise floor). See bench/README.md for the tools and the baseline layout.
