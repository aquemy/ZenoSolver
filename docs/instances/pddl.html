PDDL

The domain file, the problems zeno writes, and plane goals

MultiZenoTravel was designed as a benchmark for multi-objective temporal planners. zeno pddl writes any instance as a PDDL 2.1 problem for the domain in pddl/, with its exact front as comments, so that a planner’s output can be scored against the true front.

The domain

pddl/multi-zeno-travel.pddl (PDDL 2.1 level 3: durative actions and numeric fluents), as it stands in the repository:

; MultiZenoTravel domain (PDDL 2.1, level 3: durative actions and numeric fluents).
;
; Cities: city0 is the initial city C_I, city1..cityN are the central cities,
; city(N+1) is the goal city C_G. Every person starts in C_I and must reach C_G;
; every plane starts in C_I and must end in C_G.
;
; - fly takes (timeTerre ?from ?to) time units and adds (citythreat ?to) to the
;   total cost when the plane lands. citythreat is 0 for C_I and C_G.
; - A plane carries at most one person: (libre ?a) holds while it is empty.
; - board and debark are instantaneous. Under PDDL 2.1 epsilon-separation a plan
;   with k actions on its critical path ends at M + O(k * epsilon), where M is the
;   sum of the flight durations on that path. ZenoSolver reports M, i.e. the
;   makespan in the limit epsilon -> 0.
;
; See README.md, section "PDDL".

(define (domain multi-zeno-travel)
  (:requirements :typing :durative-actions :fluents)
  (:types city locatable - object
          aircraft person - locatable)

  (:predicates
    (at ?x - locatable ?c - city)
    (in ?p - person ?a - aircraft)
    (libre ?a - aircraft))

  (:functions
    (timeTerre ?from ?to - city)
    (citythreat ?c - city)
    (total-cost))

  (:action board
    :parameters (?p - person ?a - aircraft ?c - city)
    :precondition (and (at ?p ?c) (at ?a ?c) (libre ?a))
    :effect (and (not (at ?p ?c)) (in ?p ?a) (not (libre ?a))))

  (:action debark
    :parameters (?p - person ?a - aircraft ?c - city)
    :precondition (and (in ?p ?a) (at ?a ?c))
    :effect (and (not (in ?p ?a)) (libre ?a) (at ?p ?c)))

  (:durative-action fly
    :parameters (?a - aircraft ?from ?to - city)
    :duration (= ?duration (timeTerre ?from ?to))
    :condition (at start (at ?a ?from))
    :effect (and (at start (not (at ?a ?from)))
                 (at end (at ?a ?to))
                 (at end (increase (total-cost) (citythreat ?to))))))
  • fly lasts (timeTerre ?from ?to). Its only condition is that the plane is at ?from at the start, and it adds (citythreat ?to) to total-cost when it lands.
  • board and debark are instantaneous, and (libre ?a) holds while plane ?a is empty, which enforces capacity one.
  • The metric minimises total-time and total-cost together.

Problems

zeno pddl [instance options] [-O FILE] [--no-front]

The default file name is zeno<n>n<t>t<p>pMulti.pddl. A complete example, for \(n = 2\), \(t = 2\), \(p = 1\) (durations \((1, 2)\), costs \((2, 1)\) after the swap):

$ zeno pddl -n 2 -t 2 -p 1 -O example.pddl
(define (problem MULTIZENO-A)
  (:domain  multi-zeno-travel)
  (:objects plane1 - aircraft
            person1 person2 - person
            city0 city1 city2 city3 - city)
  (:init (= (total-cost) 0)
         (= (citythreat city0) 0)
         (= (citythreat city1) 2)
         (= (citythreat city2) 1)
         (= (citythreat city3) 0)
         
; city1
         (= (timeTerre city0 city1) 1)
         (= (timeTerre city1 city0) 1)
         (= (timeTerre city3 city1) 1)
         (= (timeTerre city1 city3) 1)
         (= (timeTerre city1 city2) 103)
         
; city2
         (= (timeTerre city0 city2) 2)
         (= (timeTerre city2 city0) 2)
         (= (timeTerre city3 city2) 2)
         (= (timeTerre city2 city3) 2)
         (= (timeTerre city2 city1) 103)
         
; plane init
         (at plane1 city0)
         (libre plane1)
         
; person init
         (at person1 city0)
         (at person2 city0)
         
)
(:goal (and 
         (at person1 city3)
         (at person2 city3)
         (at plane1 city3)
         ))
  (:metric (and (minimize (total-time)) (minimize (total-cost)))))

; Durations : 
; 1 2 
; Costs : 
; 2 1 
; Pareto points : Makespan - Cost
; 6 6
; 8 5
; 10 4
; 12 3
  • city0 is \(C_I\), city1…cityN the central cities, city(N+1) is \(C_G\); citythreat is the landing cost, 0 for both hubs.
  • timeTerre is written for both orders of every pair: \(d_i\) between \(C_I\) and \(i\), \(\bar d_i\) between \(C_G\) and \(i\), and \(\max(d_i, \bar d_i) + \max(d_j, \bar d_j) + 100\) between two central cities. Central flights are never faster than through a hub, so every problem satisfies (A1) when symmetric and (A1-NS) otherwise. They are written for every pair, so the file is quadratic in \(n\).
  • Every person and every plane starts in \(C_I\), and the goal puts all of them in \(C_G\).
  • The trailing comments list the durations (both sides when non-symmetric), the costs, and, for a symmetric instance without --no-front, the Pareto front as makespan cost lines. Note the order: makespan first, the reverse of zeno front’s cost makespan.

Plane goals

The goal requires every plane to end in \(C_G\) as well as every person. Problem files written before plane goals were added (pull request #9) only constrain the persons: planes may end anywhere, and the makespan is the last arrival. For symmetric instances under (A1) that variant has the same front (Theorem 4.9, machine-checked), so the fronts in old files remain valid. The Lean checker covers it too, for certificates of version 1 (Cert.check_sound_anywhere).

Makespan semantics

board and debark take no time in the model. Under the ε-separation of PDDL 2.1, a planner’s plan with \(k\) actions on its critical path ends at \(M + O(k\varepsilon)\), where \(M\) is the sum of the flight durations on that path. ZenoSolver reports \(M\), the makespan in the limit \(\varepsilon \to 0\). Statement 1.2 of the note argues the correspondence in both directions (a sketch, not formalised). Plans of the domain are event-ordered, and for those the front is the same (Corollary 6.10). Whether PDDL planners and VAL accept zero-duration actions is not checked.

Size

The writer is quadratic in \(n\), because of the central flights: about \(2n^2\) timeTerre lines. On an Apple M4 Max, a problem with \(n = 1000\) is written in 0.36 s and takes 46 MB. Without --no-front, zeno pddl also solves the instance first, which adds its solve time (size guidance).
