Instances with known fronts

Closed forms, the Table 1 family, and small instances with checked fronts

Some instances have fronts known independently of zeno: in closed form, or computed by an exhaustive oracle. They are the natural test cases for a planner or another solver.

The linear family (default generator)

With the default options (-C 3 -D 3) and -r r, the generator builds \(d_i = r\,(n + 1 - i)\) and \(c_i = r\,i\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\); numbering the cities by increasing duration, \(d_i = r\,i\) and \(c_i = r\,(n + 1 - i)\). Cost is affine and decreasing in duration, and the order of the cities does not matter to the front. Theorem 10.1 of the note gives the front in closed form. Let \(s = 2t - p\), \(\kappa = \lceil s/p \rceil\), \(q_0 = \lfloor s/p \rfloor\), \(\rho = s - p q_0\), and \[\Phi(T) = (p - \rho)\min(T,\ n q_0) + \rho \min(T,\ n(q_0 + 1)).\] Then the front is \[\mathcal F = \big\{\big(r\,((n+1)\,s - \Phi(T)),\ 2rT\big) : \kappa \le T \le n\kappa\big\},\] with \((n - 1)\kappa + 1\) points, every \(T\) in the range being a breakpoint. The theorem needs only (S): the front is the same whatever the central flights, and every certificate of this family has only relaxed gaps. In general, the theorem covers \(d_i = i\) and \(c_i = \alpha - \beta i\) with \(\beta > 0\) and \(\alpha \ge \beta n\).

The same in Python, by increasing cost as zeno prints it:

def linear_front(n, t, p, r=1):
    """Front of zeno -n n -t t -p p -r r (default generator), Theorem 10.1."""
    s = 2 * t - p
    q0, rho = divmod(s, p)
    kappa = -(-s // p)
    phi = lambda T: (p - rho) * min(T, n * q0) + rho * min(T, n * (q0 + 1))
    return sorted((r * ((n + 1) * s - phi(T)), 2 * r * T) for T in range(kappa, n * kappa + 1))

It agrees with zeno point for point on, among others, -n 10 -t 8 -p 3 (46 points), -n 7 -t 5 -p 4 (13), -n 5 -t 9 -p 4 -r 3 (17), -n 20 -t 6 -p 2 -r 7 (96) and -n 12 -t 13 -p 5 (56). The note’s own check compared the closed form with the link-forest DP on 336 instances and 5,844 levels.

The count matches the suite: the regimes instances have fronts of 399 (\(n = 200\), \(t = 3\), \(p = 2\)), 540 (\(n = 50\), \(t = 30\), \(p = 5\)) and 1,090 points (\(n = 100\), \(t = 60\), \(p = 10\)), each \((n-1)\kappa + 1\).

The family -n N -t N -p 2

For \(n = t = N\) and \(p = 2\): \(s = 2N - 2\), \(\kappa = q_0 = N - 1\) and \(\rho = 0\), so \(\Phi(T) = 2T\) on the range and \[\mathcal F = \{(2(N^2 - 1) - 2T,\ 2T) : N - 1 \le T \le N(N-1)\}.\] Every point has cost + makespan \(= 2(N^2 - 1)\). The front runs from \((2N(N-1),\ 2(N-1))\), the fastest, to \((2(N-1),\ 2N(N-1))\), the cheapest, and has exactly \[(N-1)^2 + 1 \text{ points}.\] zeno reproduces it for every \(N\) up to 50 (2,402 points at \(N = 50\)). The benchmark suite paper-table1 holds \(N = 3, \dots, 10\):

What the closed form does not cover

  • Offset progressions \(d_i = a + bi\) with \(a > 0\): \(G\) need not be convex and \(L < E\) occurs. With \(d = (5, 7, 9, 11)\), \(c_i = 24 - 2d_i\), \(t = 7\) and \(p = 4\), \(L(20) = 80 < E(20) = 84\).
  • The other generator families (log, sqrt, \(2^i\), staircase) have no closed form. On them the front needs the general algorithm, and a greedy schedule of the landings can miss points. Whether such a greedy is exact on the linear family is open (Conjecture 10.2); it agreed on 115 small instances.

The ends of every front

Whatever the family, under (S), Proposition 7.10 fixes the two ends of the front:

  • the fastest makespan is \(M_1 = 2\,d_{\min}\,\kappa\);
  • the cheapest cost is \(s\,c_{\min}\), reached first at makespan \(2\,d_{\rm cheap}\,\kappa\), where \(d_{\rm cheap}\) is the shortest duration among the cheapest cities.

Certificates rely on both (obligations 2 and 4 of the format).

Small instances with checked fronts

  • The 27 golden instances (suites): fronts of the exhaustive oracle, in tests/golden/fronts/.
  • The LPT counterexample, \(d = (6, 10)\), \(c = (40, 20)\), \(t = 5\), \(p = 2\): six points, (160, 80), (200, 72), (240, 64), (260, 60), (280, 56) and (320, 48). A greedy schedule misses (260, 60). This front is proved in Lean by the kernel alone, for every table of central flights (Examples.lptCounterexample_front, §5).
  • Proposition 6.5, non-symmetric: \(d = (3, 19)\), \(\bar d = (3, 11)\), \(c = (5, 1)\), \(t = 7\), \(p = 4\), no central flights. The front is (10, 90), (14, 74), (18, 68), (22, 66), (26, 52), (34, 42), (38, 36), (42, 30), (50, 18), as (cost, makespan). An exhaustive search over all plans (937 million states) gives the same front. It is the instance on which waiting is harmful: the role-load bound alone stays below the front, and two of its gaps are closed only by the search kind (Proposition 6.5).
  • Zero costs, \(n = 1\), \(d_1 = 1\), \(c_1 = 0\), \(t = 5\), \(p = 3\): the front is the single point (0, 6), and plans with more than \(s\) landings also reach it (the remark under Corollary 4.8).
