Evaluate a planner

Score a multi-objective planner against the exact Pareto fronts

Write MultiZenoTravel problems with their exact fronts, run a multi-objective planner on them, and score its runs: points found, hypervolume, epsilon indicator, impossible points.

MultiZenoTravel was designed as a benchmark for multi-objective temporal planners [KSV+13]. Here its fronts are known exactly, so a planner’s output can be scored against the true front rather than the best front any planner found. Three steps: write the problems, run the planner, score its runs with bench/tools/score_front.py.

1. Write the problems

zeno pddl writes an instance as a PDDL 2.1 problem for the domain pddl/multi-zeno-travel.pddl, and for a symmetric instance it lists the exact front in the problem’s trailing comments (PDDL). Every instance of the golden and benchmark suites is symmetric.

A sensible order for planners is the 27 golden instances first: they are tiny (\(t \le 5\)), and their fronts are also confirmed by a brute-force oracle. Then paper-table1: \(n = t\) and \(p = 2\), the sizes of the original papers’ Table 1. The other suites are sized for exact solvers, and go up to \(n = 200\) cities and \(t = 60\) passengers.

# From the repository root, after building zeno (Solver > Install).
mkdir -p problems
grep -v '^#' tests/golden/instances.txt | while IFS='|' read -r name args; do
  [ -n "$name" ] || continue
  build/release/zeno pddl ${args//@DATA@/tests/golden/data} -O "problems/golden-$name.pddl"
done
grep -v '^#' bench/instances/paper-table1.txt | while IFS='|' read -r name args; do
  [ -n "$name" ] || continue
  build/release/zeno pddl ${args//@DATA@/bench/instances/data} -O "problems/$name.pddl"
done

That writes the 35 problems in well under a second. The loops are bash; under zsh, run them with bash.

2. Run the planner

The domain is PDDL 2.1 level 3, with durative actions and numeric fluents. Its metric minimises total-time and total-cost together. Keep the output of each run apart: a directory per run holding its plans in the IPC format (0.001: (fly plane1 city0 city1) [2.000]), or a file of cost makespan lines.

Validate the plans with VAL. The scorer reads the cost and the makespan of a plan; it does not check that the plan is valid.

3. Score the runs

bench/tools/score_front.py problems/n5t5p2.pddl runs/n5t5p2/* --floor-makespan

The first argument holds the exact front: a problem file, or cost makespan lines such as the output of zeno front. Each further argument is one run, scored on its own:

  • a file of cost makespan lines;
  • an IPC plan, which counts as one point: its cost is the sum of the landing costs (citythreat) of its fly actions, read from the problem, and its makespan is the end of its last action;
  • a directory, whose files are pooled into one run.

On the homepage example (3 central cities, 3 passengers, 2 planes; front (4, 20), (14, 16), (20, 14), (24, 12), (30, 10), (36, 8)), three runs score as follows:

$ bench/tools/score_front.py example.pddl runs/a runs/b.txt runs/c.txt --floor-makespan
score_front: runs/c.txt: no plan achieves cost 3, makespan 20: check the plan with VAL, and how its makespan was measured
run         points  found     hv      eps  impossible
runs/a           3    3/6  0.8154  0.1875           0
runs/b.txt       4    2/6  0.8252  0.1875           0
runs/c.txt       2    1/6  0.2836  1.0000           1
  • runs/a is a plan for (20, 14) and the two ends of the front: three exact points.
  • runs/b.txt finds two exact points, (20, 14) and (24, 12), and two dominated ones, (6, 20) and (40, 8). It finds fewer exact points than runs/a but covers more of the front, so its hypervolume is higher. The indicators measure different things; report them all.
  • runs/c.txt claims (3, 20), which is cheaper than the cheapest plan. No plan achieves it, so either the plan is invalid or its cost or makespan was counted another way. The point is left out of hv and eps, and the exit status is 1.

--json prints one JSON object per run, to collect scores over a suite:

for p in problems/*.pddl; do
  name=$(basename "$p" .pddl)
  bench/tools/score_front.py "$p" runs/"$name"/* --floor-makespan --json
done > scores.jsonl

The indicators

Let \(F\) be the exact front and \(A\) the points of a run, both as (cost, makespan) pairs.

found
The number of points of \(F\) that the run reaches: \(|A \cap F|\), out of \(|F|\).
impossible
The points of \(A\) that no point of \(F\) weakly dominates. Since \(F\) is exact, no plan achieves them. Hypervolume and \(\varepsilon\) are computed without them.

Both other indicators work on normalized objectives. Cost and makespan are mapped to \([0, 1]\) by the ideal and nadir points of \(F\): \[ \hat c = \frac{c - c_{\min}(F)}{c_{\max}(F) - c_{\min}(F)}, \qquad \hat M = \frac{M - M_{\min}(F)}{M_{\max}(F) - M_{\min}(F)}, \] and a range of 0 (a front of one point) counts as 1.

hv
The hypervolume of \(A\) over that of \(F\), with the reference point \((\rho, \rho)\): \(\mathrm{HV}(A) / \mathrm{HV}(F)\), where \(\rho = 1 + 1/(|F| - 1)\), and \(\rho = 2\) when \(|F| = 1\). It is 1 exactly when \(A\) contains \(F\). Points beyond the reference point add no volume.
eps
The additive \(\varepsilon\) indicator: the smallest shift \(\varepsilon\) such that every point of \(F\) is weakly dominated by a point of \(A\) moved by \(\varepsilon\) in both objectives, \[ I_{\varepsilon+}(A, F) = \max_{f \in F} \min_{a \in A} \max\bigl(\hat a_c - \hat f_c,\ \hat a_M - \hat f_M\bigr). \] It is 0 exactly when \(A\) contains \(F\), and infinite for an empty run.

The reference point follows Ishibuchi et al. [IISN18]. For \(|F|\) points evenly spread on a line, \(\rho = 1 + 1/(|F| - 1)\) makes the volume that only an end of the front dominates equal to the volume that only a point between them dominates. A reference point at the nadir itself would give the two ends no volume of their own. --ref sets another \(\rho\); state it with the results, since hypervolumes computed with different reference points cannot be compared.

Makespans of PDDL plans

Durations are integers, and boarding and debarking take no time in the model, so a plan’s makespan is an integer. PDDL 2.1 planners separate dependent actions by a small \(\varepsilon\), so a plan with \(k\) actions on its critical path reports \(M + k\varepsilon\). --floor-makespan rounds makespans down, which recovers \(M\) while \(k\varepsilon < 1\). Without it, such a plan never reaches a point of the front exactly. The PDDL page gives the semantics in more detail.

What the fronts guarantee

The fronts are exact, not the best known ones. For each of the 66 instances of the benchmark suites, zeno-check, a certificate checker proved sound in Lean 4, accepts the certificate of its front (results, certificates). For each of the 27 golden instances, zeno check accepts the certificate, and a brute-force oracle recomputes the front. A run with hv = 1 has found the whole Pareto front of the instance.
