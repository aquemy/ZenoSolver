This project

The current solver zeno, the theory note doc/theory/exact-front.md, the Lean 4 formalization in lean/, the certificate format and checkers, the benchmarks and this website are the work of Alexandre Quemy, in the repository aquemy/ZenoSolver.

Attribution and fair use. Throughout the blueprint, the papers’ statements and proofs are reproduced verbatim in grey boxes marked From the original paper, with the paper, section and page, solely for scholarly comparison with the note and the formalization. That material remains its authors’ and publishers’. The blue and green boxes hold this project’s contribution.

What the note corrects

The note’s §5 and §6 and the draft erratum (doc/erratum.md) give the details. In short:

The per-PPP greedy is not optimal

Both papers fix a multiset of landings (a PPP) and schedule it greedily, longest flights first onto the least-loaded plane (EvoCOP 2015 Algorithm 1, arXiv §3.2), and claim the result is the optimal makespan of that PPP. It is not. On \(d = (6, 10)\), \(c = (40, 20)\), \(t = 5\), \(p = 2\) the 2016 solver misses the front point (260, 60) (§5). That front is checked by the Lean kernel alone (Examples.lptCounterexample_front). No polynomial per-PPP rule can be exact unless P = NP: deciding whether a multiset of landings can be flown within a makespan is strongly NP-complete (Theorem 8.1).

Proposition I holds only for the front

“Pareto-optimal plans use exactly \(2t - p\) central cities” is false for plans: with zero costs, a plan with more landings can be Pareto-optimal. What holds is the front statement: the front is the Pareto set of \((c(X), M(X))\) over multisets \(X\) of \(2t - p\) landings, with \(M(X)\) the least makespan of an admissible partition of \(X\) (Corollary 4.8, machine-checked).

Waiting can be harmful in the non-symmetric case

The arXiv paper’s non-symmetric argument assumes that no plane has to wait harmfully for a passenger. That is false, even with no central flights and under the triangle inequality (Conjecture 6.5, refuted; Proposition 6.5). The note replaces the assumption with schedulable domination (Theorem 6.9, machine-checked for every plan). An exhaustive search over role configurations with exact schedules is therefore sound, and zeno uses it to close such gaps.

The pairing argument without (A1) is incomplete

Without (A1), the arXiv pairing argument concludes “lower cost” from a set inclusion and “dominates” from per-pattern durations; both steps fail. The note proves the converse it was meant to establish under the triangle inequality (AΔ) instead (Theorem 4.10, formerly Conjecture 4.10, a written proof not yet in Lean). The front characterisation then holds under (AΔ) too (Corollary 4.12). Some hypothesis is needed: with central flights that violate both (A1) and (AΔ), a plan can beat every admissible partition (Example 4.13).

What still holds

The model, the flight patterns, the lower bound of \(2t - p\) landings, and the no-waiting construction of a plan from an admissible partition (Theorem 4.1, machine-checked). On the default generator the front is known in closed form, and the published benchmark fronts of that family are probably right despite the greedy (Conjecture 10.2, checked on 115 instances, not proved).

Honesty: what is proved, and how

The blueprint has 66 nodes. 21 are machine-checked theorems and 7 are Lean definitions. 14 have formalised parts, and 24 are not in Lean. The library has 3,675 declarations (2,112 theorems) in 42 modules (Lean v4.36.0, Mathlib v4.36.0). Apart from propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, no declaration uses another axiom: no sorry and no native_decide.

The blueprint’s badges are computed when the site is built, from collectAxioms on the Lean declarations each node lists. A node is badged machine-checked only if the declarations that formalise its whole statement are axiom-clean theorems. Declarations covering only part of a statement are listed under it, with their own badges, and the node keeps the note’s status. The generator stops if a declaration is missing or a label is not found in the note.

The compiled tools add trust in the Lean compiler and in an unverified parser (trust base).
