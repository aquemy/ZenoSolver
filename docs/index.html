ZenoSolver

Exact Pareto fronts of MultiZenoTravel planning instances, with certificates and a Lean 4 proof

MultiZenoTravel is a bi-objective planning benchmark: \(p\) planes carry \(t\) passengers from an initial city \(C_I\) to a goal city \(C_G\) through \(n\) central cities, and every plan has a total cost (landing fees) and a makespan. ZenoSolver computes the exact Pareto front of an instance, so that multi-objective planners can be scored against the true front.

zeno -n 50 -t 30 -p 5            # 50 central cities, 30 passengers, 5 planes
zeno -n 2 -t 5 -p 2 -d durations-and-costs.dat --certificate front.cert
zeno check front.cert            # verify the certificate without trusting the solver

zeno prints the front as cost makespan lines by increasing cost. It is exact for symmetric durations, which includes every instance the generator emits. For non-symmetric durations it prints a certified bracket, and says whether the bracket closes into the exact front.

How it works

The front is the set of breakpoints of \(E(T)\), the least cost of an admissible partition of the \(2t-p\) landings into \(p\) groups whose durations each sum to at most \(T\) (Proposition 7.1). zeno brackets \(E\) between a relaxation \(L\) and the pairs and chains bounds \(U\), \(U_3\) on every level, and runs the exact link-forest dynamic program only where the bracket leaves a level open. The algorithm page has the details, and the theory note the proofs.

The 2016 ZenoSolver, published with the EvoCOP 2015 paper, scheduled each fixed multiset of landings greedily, and that greedy is not optimal (§5). The current solver replaces it and reproduces every golden front of an exhaustive oracle, including those on which the 2016 solvers were wrong.

On the 21 instances that the 2016 zenoSolver finishes, zeno is 10 times faster at the median and up to 8,414 times faster (paper-table1/n8t8p2). Process start-up, about 3 ms, is the floor of every zeno timing.

What is proved

The blueprint has 63 nodes. 21 are machine-checked theorems and 7 are Lean definitions. 13 have formalised parts, and 22 are not in Lean. The library has 3,675 declarations (2,112 theorems) in 42 modules (Lean v4.36.0, Mathlib v4.36.0). Apart from propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, no declaration uses another axiom: no sorry and no native_decide.

The front theorem is machine-checked for every instance with symmetric durations and central flights no faster than through a hub, assumptions (S) and (A1) (Corollary 4.8). So are the exactness of both dynamic programs (Theorem 7.5, Theorem 7.6), the verified solver zeno-front (solveFront_isParetoFront) and the soundness of the certificate checker zeno-check, symmetric (Theorem 9.1) and not (version 2). For non-symmetric instances the note proves schedulable domination (Theorem 6.9, machine-checked). The exhaustive search over role configurations that closes the gaps left by harmful waiting (Proposition 6.5) rests on it.

Who did what. The model and the first algorithms are those of the original papers, Quemy & Schoenauer (EvoCOP 2015) and Quemy, Schoenauer & Dréo (arXiv:2304.14659, 2023). The current solver, the theory note that corrects the papers, the Lean 4 formalization and this website are the work of Alexandre Quemy. On the blueprint, the papers’ statements are reproduced verbatim in grey boxes with their attribution; blue and green boxes hold the note and the Lean development.
