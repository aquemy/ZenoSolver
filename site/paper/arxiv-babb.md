**From the original paper** (Quemy, Schoenauer & Dreo, arXiv:2304.14659v1, §3.3 *Adapting the method to $B\bar{A}\bar{B}$ situations*).

> Consider an instance $\Pi$ of the non-symmetric MultiZenoTravel problem. For each potential
> $B\bar{A}\bar{B}$ situation going through $C_{i}$ and $C_{j}$, add a new city $C_{k}$ such that:
> $$d_{k}^{B}=2d_{i} \quad (3) \qquad d_{k}^{\bar{B}}=2d_{j} \quad (4) \qquad d_{k}^{A}=\min(d_{i}+\bar{d}_{i},d_{j}+\bar{d}_{j}) \quad (5) \qquad d_{k}^{\bar{A}}=\bar{d}_{i}+d_{ij}+d_{j} \quad (6)$$
> This transformation is illustrated by Figure 10. The transformation has $\mathcal{O}(n^{2})$
> complexity and the resulting instance $\Pi^{*}$ is a non-symmetric MultiZenoTravel without any
> $B\bar{A}\bar{B}$ situation.
